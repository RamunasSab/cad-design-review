# Plastic Moldings: ISO 20457 / DIN 16742 Tolerances

Use this guide for `injection_molding`. Numerical data is stored in `tolerance-data.json` under `iso20457`; query it with `scripts/check_tolerances.py plastic ...`. Do not estimate table values from memory.

## 1. Standard status
- DIN 16742:2013-10 is withdrawn, although its tables are still cited in drawings.
- ISO 20457:2018 was the first edition based on DIN 16742.
- ISO 20457:2026 is the second edition, published in August 2026, and is the current edition.
- Values in this skill are DIN 16742:2013 tables for sizes 1–315 mm, reproduced from the available source summary. They have not been verified against the full ISO 20457:2026 text.
- Reports must state: “TG values are based on DIN 16742:2013 / reproduced ISO 20457 tables and have not been verified against the full ISO 20457:2026 text. Confirm critical dimensions and contractual requirements against the applicable edition.”
- The 2026 edition description refers to acceptance conditions and changes related to the ISO 8015 default drawing condition for the plastics industry. Verify interpretation in the full standard; do not infer details from the summary alone.

## 2. Terms
- **Tolerance groups TG1–TG9:** higher group numbers generally mean looser tolerances; TG1 is tightest.
- **W (tool-specific):** dimension is determined by the mold tool; use the tighter table value.
- **NW (non-tool-specific):** dimension is not determined solely by the mold tool; use the looser value.
- **Production series 1–4:** series 1 is normal production and the basis for general tolerances; series 2 is fine, 3 precision, and 4 special precision. Series 3 and 4 require agreement between customer and molder.
- Values are symmetric (±). Sizes below 1 mm or above 315 mm are not covered and must be marked `NEEDS_CLARIFICATION`.

## 3. Review procedure
1. The title block should state `General tolerances ISO 20457 - TGx` (older drawings may cite DIN 16742 - TGx). If absent, unspecified dimensions are ambiguous. If ISO 2768 is applied to a molded plastic part, flag a `RISK`.
2. TG6 is common as a supplier default, but critical features may require TG5 or tighter. An unnecessarily tight group increases tooling and inspection cost.
3. Use `plastic --find --size X --tol T --kind W|NW` to identify the TG associated with a direct tolerance. TG1–TG3 may require higher production series and supplier agreement. Values tighter than TG1 or outside table coverage require separate agreement.
4. The standard determines W/NW classification, but the available source summary does not provide complete criteria. As a practical interpretation, a dimension formed by one mold half may be W; a dimension crossing the parting line, moving slide, or secondary operation may be NW. If uncertain, show both values and mark `NEEDS_CLARIFICATION`.
5. ISO 286 fits (H7, g6, etc.) on plastic features are a `RISK`; they are intended for metal. They may be suitable for metal inserts or mating metal features.
6. Tight tolerances without material shrinkage and warpage data are a `RISK`.
7. Parting lines, moving mold components, and secondary operations can reduce achievable accuracy. Discuss critical dimensions with the molder early.

## 4. Commands
```bash
python scripts/check_tolerances.py plastic --tg 6 --size 40
python scripts/check_tolerances.py plastic --tg 5 --size 100 --kind NW
python scripts/check_tolerances.py plastic --find --size 40 --tol 0.15 --kind W
python scripts/check_tolerances.py plastic --series 1
```

## 5. Data limitations
The source does not provide sizes below 1 mm or above 315 mm, position/profile/form tolerances, a complete TG selection method, material/process group assignment A–F, or complete W/NW classification criteria. The full ISO 20457:2026 text is not included.
